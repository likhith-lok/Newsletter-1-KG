DP2 students explore Amsterdam on 5-day excursion 

 

From 21 to 25 September the entire DP2 year group went on a 5-day excursion to Amsterdam in the Netherlands. The students travelled by bus from Denmark, they stayed in a hostel located in the northern part of the city. Throughout the week they followed a structured program that included cultural activities, environmental education and opportunities to meet new people. The excursion allowed students to explore Amsterdam, learn about its history and spend time together outside the classroom. 

One of the parts of the program was visiting museums. The entire group visited the Rijksmuseum, where students took part in guided tours and learned about art and history. In addition, students could choose between three museums according to their personal interests: the Van Gogh Museum, the Maritime Museum and the World Museum. This gave everyone the opportunity to experience different aspects of Dutch culture. 

Another important activity was a guided walking tour at Oostvaardersplassen, a nature reserve known for its rewilding project. During the tour students learned about the area and the importance of allowing nature to develop with human intervention. The visit provided an opportunity to understand how natural ecosystems can be protected. It is also connected to environmental topics that students study. 

The excursion also included a guided bicycle tour around Amsterdam. Cycling is a part of everyday life in the Netherlands and the tour allowed students to experience the city in a way that is familiar to local population. Riding through the streets gave them an opportunity to explore parts of Amsterdam while observing its architecture, canals and busy cycling culture. Combining activity with sightseeing made the experience both educational and enjoyable. 

To find out more about the students experiences we interviewed two DP2 students. Asked them about their favorite parts of the excursion. Both students identified the guided bicycle tour as one of the highlights of the trip. One student explained that cycling around Amsterdam was her activity because it allowed her to explore the city in a different way. She enjoyed the opportunity to see more of the city while learning about its surroundings from the guide.  Her response highlights how practical activities can make educational excursions more engaging. Instead of learning about Amsterdam in a classroom students could experience the city directly.  

In addition to environmental activities the trip included a social activity with students from the International School of Amsterdam. During a Theory of Knowledge (TOK) class students from both schools had the opportunity to meet, communicate and share their experiences. This encouraged cooperation and allowed students to connect with others who are also studying the IB Diploma Programme. Another part of the exchange involved introducing the Danish dance, Les Lanciers to students from the International School of Amsterdam.  

Overall, the 5-day excursion offered DP2 students a combination of education, cultural exploration and social interaction. From visiting museums and learning about rewilding to cycling through Amsterdam and meeting students the program provided experiences beyond the classroom. The trip also encouraged independence, collaboration and stronger connections, within the year group. It was an opportunity for students to develop both personally and academically while discovering a new city.
