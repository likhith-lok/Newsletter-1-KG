Life After IB: What Next?

With IAs and exams to complete, it can be hard to think about university. But does the IB really help once you get there? We spoke to two ex-IB students from Kolding Gymnasium.

One graduated 3 years ago and is currently in their second year of Mechatronics Engineering at SDU Sønderborg. The other graduated last year and is studying Psychology, International Relations and Philosophy at the University of St Andrews.

Choosing an option

The engineering student knew what he wanted to do. He had always wanted to do engineering and SDU offered the course he wanted in English.

The St Andrews student enjoyed having more time to choose her area. At her university, she can focus on three subjects in her first two years and then specialize in one or two.

Was IB useful?

Both agreed that they found it useful. The engineering student found his Maths AA HL and Physics HL very useful. Whilst they were difficult subjects, they prepared him for the maths he is using now. He finds the skill of applying knowledge to new problems very useful.

The St Andrews student has found most of her IB subjects useful. She uses her history and ESS topics in her International Relations subject. Maths is useful for the statistics in her Psychology. She even gets to use her Spanish with two of her flatmates!

Her EE, IAs and TOK have also been useful. She has found that her essays are like “mini-EE’s”. Her Psychology lab report feels similar to her Psychology IA. As she has done this before it has made things easier.

The engineering student hasn’t found it as linked. However, knowing how to structure information, reference and explain how something works has helped him with his project reports.

Is it more stressful?

The two had very different experiences. The engineering student doesn’t have as many classes each year and doesn’t have as much to learn. Whilst the concepts are more advanced, the exams aren’t as stressful. Overall he feels it is “less stress”

The St Andrews student has found it more independent. Whilst the IB gives you a syllabus to know what to learn, at university she has lectures and multiple texts to go through. She has to decide what the most important information is.

What should current IB students know?

The engineering student would have liked to have started his assignments earlier. He would recommend doing past papers under timed conditions and learning from your mistakes. Also try to research the universities you want to go to before DP2 as you will have less time due to deadlines and exam prep.

The St Andrews student would recommend being specific with your revision. Instead of just saying you will revise maths on Friday, pick a topic and commit to doing past paper questions on that topic. It is also useful to look back on your results and think if your methods actually worked.

She would have liked to have not stressed so much over everything in IB. Try to do one assignment at a time and remind yourself that you can complete it.

If you had to describe IB in three words, the engineering student would say “demanding, worthwhile, growth.” whilst the St Andrews student would say “enriching, comprehensive and applicable”

It’s lovely to hear from people who have got through! Whilst we may not enjoy the deadlines right now, some of the work can make things a bit easier moving forward.
