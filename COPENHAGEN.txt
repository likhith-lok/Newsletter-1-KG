Draft 2

DAY 1 
The Copenhagen trip started with excitement as the students were going to the capital of Denmark for 3 days. This excursion provided a opportunity for students to socialize , strengthen friendships and build connections with classmates outside the school environment.

Upon arrival, the students explored Rådhuspladsen before visiting the Museum of illusions. The museum offered an entertaining introduction to the science of perception through interactive displays, including an upside-down house and an illusionary bridge that made visitors feel as though their surroundings were moving. These activities encouraged students to question what they saw and experience how perception can differ from reality.

The day continued at the Copenhagen International Museum, where students explored historical objects and cultural exhibits from different parts of the world. Working in groups, they searched for items representing different regions and documented their discoveries through photographs. The visit highlighted the diversity of human history and traditions, allowing students to appreciate cultures beyond their own.

Overall, the first day combined entertainment with cultural learning. Through shared activities and group exploration, students began building stronger relationships while discovering new perspectives on the world.

Day 2: Politics, History and a Canal Adventure

The second day focused on Denmark’s political system, its history and the opportunity to experience Copenhagen from a different perspective. Following breakfast, the students visited the Danish Parliament, where they met Mr Benny Engelbrecht, a Member of Parliament and former Minister for Transport.

During the guided tour, students learned about Denmark’s political and legal systems, as well as the historical significance of the parliament’s artwork and sculptures. They also explored a historic room that offered insight into parliamentary traditions and political discussions of the past.

One of the most valuable parts of the visit was the opportunity to ask Mr Engelbrecht questions directly. This allowed students interested in politics and law to gain a better understanding of public service and political participation. The visit also provided an opportunity to compare different political systems and understand the importance of public institutions in society.

Following this educational experience, the students took a canal tour of Copenhagen. The 45-minute boat ride offered beautiful views of the city from the water, revealing its architecture and distinctive waterfront scenery. The relaxing journey provided a different way to appreciate the Danish capital and brought the day’s activities to a memorable conclusion.

Day 3: Reflection and Lasting Memories

The final morning offered students a last opportunity to explore Copenhagen before beginning their journey back to Kolding. Some visited local landmarks, while others enjoyed walks around the city, making the most of their remaining time in the capital.

As the return journey began, the atmosphere on the train was noticeably different from the excitement of the outward trip. The lively conversations and games had largely been replaced by quiet discussions and sleeping passengers, reflecting the exhaustion that followed three days of activities and exploration.

Upon returning to Kolding, the students went their separate ways, bringing the excursion to an end. However, the value of the trip extended beyond the places they had visited. It provided opportunities to develop friendships, learn about Danish history and culture, and gain a deeper appreciation of the different backgrounds represented among their classmates.

For international students in particular, the excursion offered a chance to become more familiar with their host country. It also demonstrated how the IB experience extends beyond academic lessons, encouraging cultural awareness, collaboration and learning through shared experiences.

Ultimately, the Copenhagen trip brought together education, exploration and friendship, leaving students with a broader understanding of Denmark and stronger connections with one another.
